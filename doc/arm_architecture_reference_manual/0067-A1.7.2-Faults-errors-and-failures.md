# ​A1.7.2 Faults, errors, and failures

##### A1.7.2 Faults, errors, and failures

R NVNNC
:   Correct service is delivered when the service implements the system function.

I QWSVK
:   [Correct service](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-2-Faults--errors--and-failures?lang=en#mdterm_correct_service) might include:

    - Producing correct results.
    - Producing results within the time allotted to the task.
    - Not divulging secret or secure information.

R KRTSC
:   For the purpose of describing the RAS Extension and RAS System Architecture, deviation from [correct service](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-2-Faults--errors--and-failures?lang=en#mdterm_correct_service) is defined using the following terms:

    - A failure is the event of deviation from correct service. This includes data corruption, data loss, and service loss.
    - An error is the deviation from correct service. An incorrect value that has an [error](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-2-Faults--errors--and-failures?lang=en#mdterm_ras_error) is corrupt.
    - A fault is the cause of the error.

R JNBDX
:   Errors that are present but not detected are latent errors or undetected errors.

I TNQPK
:   In a system with no error detection, all errors are [latent errors](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-2-Faults--errors--and-failures?lang=en#mdterm_ras_latent_error) and are [silently propagated](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-3-General-taxonomy-of-errors?lang=en#mdterm_ras_silently_propagate) by components until they are either masked or cause [failure](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-2-Faults--errors--and-failures?lang=en#mdterm_ras_failure).

I GRYKV
:   The severity of a [failure](/documentation/ddi0487/mc/-Part-A-Arm-Architecture-Introduction-and-Overview/-Chapter-A1-Introduction-to-the-Arm-Architecture/-A1-7-Reliability--Availability--and-Serviceability/-A1-7-2-Faults--errors--and-failures?lang=en#mdterm_ras_failure) can range from minor to catastrophic:

    - The harmful consequences of a minor failure are of a similar cost to the benefits provided by correct service delivery.
    - The harmful consequences of a catastrophic failure are orders of magnitude, or even incommensurably, higher than the benefit provided by correct service delivery.

I NMGPQ
:   There are many sources of faults in a system, including both software and hardware faults:

    - *Hardware faults* originate in, or affect, hardware.
    - *Software faults* affect software, that is programs or data.

    The RAS Extension and RAS System Architecture primarily address errors produced from hardware faults. These fall into two main areas:

    - Transient faults.
    - Non-transient or *persistent faults*.
